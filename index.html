The condition of a home on the day you move in, and the day you move out, is what deposit, handover and repair disputes come down to. This check walks every room, records the condition with time-stamped photos, keeps meter readings and keys, and compares move-in with move-out, item by item. Use it as a tenant, a landlord, or a buyer on the final walk-through before completion.

Before you start - safety first

HSE
If you smell gas: get fresh air, open doors and windows, turn off the emergency control valve if it is safe, no flames or electrical switches, and call the gas emergency number from a safe place. (Gas Safe Register)
  • Test smoke alarms on the day you move in; NFPA advises testing them at least once a month. (NFPA)
  • Where fuel-burning appliances are fitted, check for a battery-operated or battery back-up CO alarm. (CDC Carbon Monoxide)
  • Look only at damaged sockets, switches or exposed wiring. Do not touch them; report them in writing. (Professional practice)
  • Take photos from the floor. Do not stand on furniture, benches or baths to reach high areas. (Professional practice)

    How priorities are defined

    SHEET 06 · KEY
    HighSafety items only: a smoke or CO alarm not working, a gas smell, or exposed wiring or damaged sockets.
    MediumAnything damaged, missing, dirty, not working or changed since move-in, and any evidence still to collect (photos, meter readings, keys, signed report).
    LowRecorded for completeness, with no issue found.

    Priority organises your record. It is not a legal finding on who is responsible. Priority definitions and condition words: professional practice.

    What this tool will not do

    LIMITS
    • It does not decide who pays for damage or how much of a deposit is returned. Your tenancy agreement, local law, the deposit scheme or a tribunal do that.
    • It does not replace an official condition report or inventory form required in your country or state. Use it alongside that form.
    • It is not a building survey. For hidden defects, use the other Beneath the Surface checks.

    Standards & Measurements

    REFERENCES

    Guidance used in this tool

    • Inventory and photos (England): check the inventory against the property on moving day and ask for it to be corrected before you sign; take your own photos of any marks, even if listed; sign and date it only when accurate and keep a copy; photograph the meters when you move in. (MoneyHelper Moving checklist, Shelter Inventory)
    • Deposit (England): the landlord or agent must put the deposit in a government-approved scheme within 30 days of getting it, and give you the required information within 30 days. The scheme offers a free dispute service. (UK Gov Deposit protection)
    • Moving out (England): clean to the same standard as when you moved in, take final meter readings on the day you move out, hand your keys back, try to be there for any check-out inspection, and raise a dispute with the deposit scheme if you disagree with deductions. (Shelter Moving out, MoneyHelper Moving checklist, Shelter Inventory)
    • Condition report (NSW, Australia): the landlord or agent completes a condition report before the tenant moves in; the tenant completes their part and returns a copy within 7 days, keeping the other copy; photos with time stamps are encouraged. (NSW Condition reports)
    • Fair wear and tear vs damage (NSW, Australia): for example, faded curtains or frayed cords vs missing or torn curtains; furniture indentations and traffic marks vs stains or burns in carpet; faded, chipped or cracked paint vs an unapproved paint job; worn benchtop vs burns or cuts; loose hinges or worn sliding tracks vs broken glass. (NSW Fair wear and tear)
    • Buying (US): before closing, inspect the home and make sure it is acceptable and any agreed repairs have been completed. (CFPB)
    • Safety at handover: test smoke alarms at least once a month; install a battery-operated or battery back-up CO detector; know the gas emergency steps. (NFPA, CDC Carbon Monoxide, Gas Safe Emergency)
    • Units: 1 cm = 0.39 in. Meter readings as shown on the meter, with the unit printed on it.
    Country-specific: deposit protection, condition report forms, deadlines (for example 7 days in NSW), pre-move-out inspections and what counts as fair wear and tear are set by local law and differ between countries and states. Check your tenancy agreement and your local tenancy authority. Buyers: check your contract for the final inspection and handover terms.

    Items marked Professional practice reflect general inspection and HSE practice. No single public standard covers them, so no standard is claimed.

      Before you start: how your information is saved

      Your information is saved on this device only.

      Save a backup file regularly.

      Where is my information saved?

      Inside this browser, on this device. Nothing is sent to The Snag Tag or anyone else. That keeps it private, but it also means you are the only one who can keep a copy safe.

      Why could my information seem to disappear?
      • You open the tool in a different browser, a private/incognito window, or on another device.
      • You open a different copy of the tool (for example a downloaded file instead of your link).
      • Your browsing history or site data gets cleared.
      • Safari (iPhone, iPad and Mac) can delete saved information if you don't open the tool for about 7 days.
      • The tool is open in two tabs at the same time. Always use one tab.
      How do I keep my information safe?
      • Press Save a backup now after every session where you change something.
      • Keep the backup file somewhere safe, like your cloud drive or a USB stick.
      • Always open the tool from the same link, in the same browser.
      How do I get my information back, or use another device?

      Open the tool, press Restore a backup and choose your latest backup file. Everything comes back as it was when you saved it.
